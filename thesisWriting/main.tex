\abstract
In recent years, Graphic Processing Units (GPUs) and their embedded hardware accelerators have become central to high-performance computing, particularly for deep learning and matrix-intensive workloads. A significant part of their efficiency comes from dedicated compute units that support different numerical formats directly in hardware. While traditional floating-point and integer representations remain dominant, emerging formats such as Floating Point 8 (FP8), logarithmic numbers, and posit representations are increasingly considered as alternatives. However, the architectural implications and practical benefits of integrating these formats into accelerator pipelines are not yet fully understood.

This thesis investigates the effectiveness of alternative number formats through the design and integration of multi-precision tensor compute units (TCUs) along their surrounding elements. The work begins with a study of tensor core architectures and data processing units, followed by the RTL-level development of arithmetic structures supporting multiply and accumulate (MAC) operations in different numerical representations. A generic multi-precision compute core is implemented and validated against golden reference models to ensure functional correctness.

The developed accelerator is then integrated into both a GPU core and a RISC-V processor model, requiring the definition of new instruction extensions and control logic for matrix operations. Finally, the system is evaluated under various numerical formats to examine performance behavior and resilience characteristics in deep neural network workloads.

The results aim to clarify the trade-offs between precision, architectural complexity, and computational robustness, contributing to the design of future hardware accelerators supporting emerging number formats.
